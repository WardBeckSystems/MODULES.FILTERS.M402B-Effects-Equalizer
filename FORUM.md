> Hello.
>
> Size is not an issue in either case. The axial caps will be smaller in diameter and shorter in length - that's progress. As far as the radial ones (with the leads coming from the same said on the can) spacing is not crucial. As technology has progressed the caps have become smaller, thus the leads (centre to centre) are closer together. With a little teflon tubing you can stretch the new leads to go further apart. It is advisable to have one lead un-molested, and the other lead will be bent (re-formed) and encapsulated with teflon tubing to span the distance to the new holes in the PCB. Common practice in prototyping.

## Help restoring my WBS 1204

_WBS TECHNICAL SUPPORT · Feb 15, 2012|45_

> Hello.
>
> Well the LM201 to 301 will be a HUGE change. I advise putting the op-amps in sockets unless they are already in there. Do not use the silly machined sockets - use dual wiper gas-less seal AMP sockets. They will make service better for you in the long run.
>
> Exploring all the sick modules are good. I doubt the 4 electrolytics on the modules are bad. I would pull the daughter boards, test the base module for voltage - take the daughter boards form the sick modules and plug them one by one in to a healthy module to further isolate who is sick of the bunch. On the M461's could be a 4-110 board, on the EQ's could be an 8-109 (4-109 depending upon vintage), etc... this will help narrow down what is bad, and eliminate expensive tech time - as they charge by the hour!! Also by pulling the daughter boards gently crimp the pins with needle nose pliers to make them a little more compressive/stiff. THat will help in making a secure connection - as we do not know what the CBC or the TV station did over the years with this board. 
>
> Most likely they are a sick LM201 - they do die over time!! I had one that had cracked in half in one of my M402B's. Heat is an enemy!!!
>
> You are 70% there. This business above YOU should be able to do - and let the tech replace the op-amps.
>
> For the power-supplies have the tech do the m first. THe computer grade caps most likely will not need replacing - but Mouser, Digi Key, and Electrosonic all have the appropriate caps in stock and they still fit, etc...
>
> Good luck and let us know of your developments.

— **kuzub**, Jun 11, 2012|45

> Well hang-on regarding cleaning. They are are ceramic plates with silver contacts and are designed to be self cleaning. I own (2) M402B's and are 100% perfect and I have never touched the switches. I also have (2) M402 straights and their switches are 100% as well. Ba-ti-dah I know ..... but the contacts are self cleaning so unless there is some intermittancy that you find in rotating them - like a sweet spot where they work, then I would not look as dirt/oxidation being the culprit (but a brief spray with WD40 and a few turns never hurt - however if they were dirty a few turns would have solved the issue). Open the EQ and check the solder terminals and freshen them up on the defective switch first. Then if the issue persists - determine if the entire switch gives you grief or just 1 or 2 frequencies. If 1 or 2 frequencies then the issue is a sick component. If the entire switch is still funkie, then trace the wires to the PCB at the send to the switch and the return from the switch and re-solder those wire points. 
>
> Secondly if you still have grief measure the bias of the op-amps. LM201's. You might have a sick one as they are from the very early 1970's and I had one crack open on em once giving me a high noise floor.
>
> The EQ engagement push-button switch will not introduce noise to the circuit.
>
> Hope this assists.

## M480 Pad

_WBS TECHNICAL SUPPORT · Dec 21, 2012|45_

> Well as evolution did tis thing the LM201 became the LM301. The LM301 underwent a few changes over the years, and both components were in production by National Semi-Conductor, and Texas Instruments (maybe 1or 2 others as well) for many years. We used both components interchangeably due to availability. But he "newer" LM301 became the more common component going forward in our production cycles. IF you have a module with an LM201 and you drop in an LM301 that you bought lets say today, you will not only measure a difference, I would argue that in a quiet studio, you will be able to hear the difference (listen to the thermal noise generated by the unit when on). I feel that on my first T1202A I could hear it on the M480B modules, I believe I am able to hear it on my M460 modules from the 70's and even the ones ion the 80's. AND to further this - I had a sick M402B a few years ago. dad and I opened it up and found the bad op-amp - an LM201. It really failed. On the scope the noise was a consistent rumble, ever present and very obvious when compared to the modules twin. So we pulled the IC, which was split on the EQ's PCB, but still working! Dad dropped in a new LM301, and of course the EQ worked and behaved quite nicely. Then we compared it to the twin 402 eq. Dad's eyebrows shot up, and he replaced the op-amps in that EQ as well. For fun I am refurbishing 20 odd M460 modules for my console, but leaving 2 modules 100% un-touched. When this project is done, I will explore the differences in 4-109/8-109 cards, and carious voltage regulator and op-amp configurations. 
>
> But yes, I believe the change form the old LM301 to modern day LM301's will net you a small, measurable change. And if you ran all modules open - the noise floor as a whole, will be measurably lower.
>
> Regards,
>
> Warren
